How it works

Your iPhone saves where each photo was taken and which way the camera faced. Toposcope reads that from the photo, draws the terrain as it looks from that exact spot (out to 220 km, with Earth curvature and refraction), and then Skyline Lock slides and scales that drawing until it sits on the real skyline in your photo. Each summit gets its name, height and distance.

Phone compasses are often tens of degrees off. Skyline Lock doesn't trust the compass: it uses the shape of the skyline itself. It even works for photos that have no compass heading at all.

Questions

My photo says "This photo has no location". The photo has no GPS data (for example a screenshot, a photo sent through some messaging apps, or a camera without GPS). Tap Choose on the map and put the cross where you stood.

The labels are a little off. Tap Align, drag the photo until the cyan ridge line sits on the real skyline, and pinch to change the field of view. Lock again re-runs Skyline Lock from your position.

"Couldn't lock onto the skyline". Clouds on the summits, fog, or a photo where trees or buildings hide the skyline can stop the match. Align by hand as above.

Does it work offline? The included examples always do. Everywhere else Toposcope downloads the terrain the first time you look from a new place (about 10 MB) and keeps it in a cache. With Pro you can download whole areas before a trip in Settings → Offline areas.

Which peaks are included? Every named summit in OpenStreetMap: over a million worldwide. If a name is missing or wrong, it can be fixed on openstreetmap.org and reaches Toposcope with the next data update.

Live camera. Live mode needs a device with a camera and uses the compass. Tap Skyline Lock to correct the compass from what the camera sees.

Restore a purchase. Settings → Restore purchases, signed in with the same Apple Account.

Safety

Peak names, heights and the terrain model can contain errors. Toposcope is not a navigation aid: in the mountains, use a proper map, check the weather and rely on your own judgement.
